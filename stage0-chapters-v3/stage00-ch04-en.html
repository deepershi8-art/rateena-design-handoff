CHAPTER 04

Two Facilities, One Standard of Excellence

The project makes its audience a simple promise:

Whatever you need in sound, in any of its forms, you'll find it in these two facilities.

International stars and Arab pop artists. Emerging musicians. Mahraganat and rap performers. Film sound-effects specialists. Quran reciters. Podcast creators and hosts. Voice-over artists working on news and documentaries.

If you haven't come across the word, Foley is the art of performing and recording everyday sounds in sync with the picture: footsteps, the rustle of clothes, doors opening and closing, and other physical actions.

All of these people and crafts will be served across two separate facilities, to the same technical standard.

This study is only about the world-class flagship recording studio, but it's worth introducing the other facilities we plan to build as part of the wider project.

The world-class flagship recording studio and the integrated audio center are two separate places. The flagship studio stands on its own. The audio center holds everything else, along with the Audio Research Laboratory.

The reason is simple. The flagship studio's guests are celebrities and big-name artists who need privacy and comfort. They don't want fans, or the other people who come to record at the audio center, downstairs or in the same building.

The Audio Research Laboratory

Alongside the recording and production rooms, we plan to set up a dedicated audio research laboratory at the audio center. It's linked to the Flagship Studio, but physically separate from it.

The laboratory won't run as a commercial recording studio.

It's a place for research, experiments and the study of sound itself, so we can understand audio technology more deeply.

Think of it as an extension of the flagship studio's scientific and technical work, kept apart from day-to-day recording and production.

Why Start With the Flagship Studio?

Because the flagship sets the bar for quality and performance across the whole project.

The decisions we make for the world-class flagship studio, about sound isolation, air-conditioning noise, clean power, cabling, and how signals travel through the building, will set the technical standard for the entire audio center.

The other rooms will inherit that standard, each according to its size, purpose and needs.

If we get the flagship studio right, the second phase is mostly a careful reuse of decisions we've already proven, instead of having the same debates from scratch for every room.

We recommend building the world-class flagship recording studio first, at its own dedicated location, separate from the main audio center, according to the project's technical requirements.
