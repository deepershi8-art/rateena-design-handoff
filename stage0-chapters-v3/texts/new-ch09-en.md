## Chapter 9: Glossary of Technical Terms

We'll use a lot of technical terms in this study, and some of them may be new to you.

So here's a simple glossary of the essentials to start with. We'll keep adding to it as we go.

### 1. Airborne Noise
Sound that travels from one place to another through the air.
Car horns, people talking, loud music, street noise: anything that reaches us through the air.

### 2. Structure-borne Noise
Noise that travels as vibration through solid materials and through the structure of the building itself.
For example, vibrations from a metro train or heavy machinery can travel through the ground, the foundations and the walls, and reach the studio even if it is well isolated against airborne sound.

### 3. Isolation
Isolation is the set of methods and techniques we use to prevent, or reduce to the required degree, the transmission of sound and vibration from one room to another, or from outside the building to the inside, and the reverse.

### 4. Room Acoustics
This isn't the same as isolation.
Isolation deals with the sound coming into or going out of the room.
Room acoustics studies the sound once it is already inside the room itself.
How does it reflect off the walls, ceiling and floor? How is it distributed? How does it decay over time? And how can we control its behaviour to get the result we want?

### 5. HVAC
Short for Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning.
It's the system that heats, ventilates and cools the building.
In a studio it can be one of the biggest sources of noise, even though it's only there to keep people comfortable.
Design it like ordinary office air conditioning and you may find, too late, that the sound of air, fans and vibration is ruining your recordings.

### 6. Flagship Studio
The studio with the highest technical spec and performance in the RATEENA SOUND project.
It's the reference room that sets the quality bar for everything else in the project.

### 7. Foley
Foley is the art of performing and recording everyday sound effects in sync with the picture.
Footsteps, the rustle of clothes, doors opening, things being moved, and all the other little sounds we hear in films.
A Foley artist can re-create these sounds in a dedicated studio, so that they sync precisely with the movement of the characters and objects on screen.

### 8. Room-within-a-Room
Also called Box-in-Box.
This is a construction method for sound isolation in which we build an independent inner shell inside the main structure of the building.
This inner shell has its own isolated walls, ceiling and floor, and is separated from the outer structure by air gaps and isolating and support elements designed to reduce the transmission of vibration.
Picture a box sitting inside a bigger box, with nothing rigid joining the two.
The idea is to reduce the transmission of sound and vibration between the room and the outside world.

### 9. Flanking Path
Sometimes you build a wall with excellent isolation, and the sound simply finds another way around it.
For example, through a shared floor, a continuous ceiling, an air-conditioning duct, a pipe, or a structural void.
We call this alternative route a Flanking Path.
In other words, sound doesn't have to go through the wall. It can sneak around it through other parts of the building.

### 10. Plant / Plant Room
This refers to the mechanical equipment that serves the building, such as chillers, air-handling units, pumps, and electrical generators.
The Plant Room is the room or space where this equipment is housed.
The building can't run without it, but it can also make noise and vibration. That's why where it goes, and how it's isolated, are big decisions in studio design.
