## Who Is This Story For?

I wrote this study for serious, smart businesspeople and investors who may not have a technical background in sound, who don't spend their days in recording studios, and who've probably never wondered what's inside a studio wall.

As an investor, you don't need to know what a compressor does. You don't need to follow every argument audio engineers have about cables.

What you need is much simpler:

What do we need, and why do we need it?

This study is meant to show you clearly where your money goes, and what it actually builds.

At the same time, I've written it so that a professional audio engineer, even one who's never helped build a facility this big, can follow every technical decision without guessing at the jargon.

This is a technical study. It isn't a feasibility study or a business plan, and it doesn't try to work out whether the center or the studio will make money.

It answers a narrower, harder, more specialized question:

What exactly does it take to build a truly world-class, fully integrated music and audio recording facility?

That means understanding the rooms, the different ways to isolate sound, room acoustics, infrastructure, equipment categories, and how the place is run.

We'll start with the world-class flagship recording studio, in full technical detail, and sketch out the other facilities of the integrated audio center so we can come back to them later.

The first phase ends by setting the professional fees for the study and proposing a second phase, which takes the same technical depth to the rest of the audio complex.
