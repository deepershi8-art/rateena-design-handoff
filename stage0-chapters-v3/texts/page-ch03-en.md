## What Does “World-Class” Really Mean?

Being world-class in music and audio recording isn't about a slogan on the studio door that says, "We're not the only ones, but we're the best."

It means an artist or producer who's worked in London, Los Angeles, New York or Paris can come to Cairo, walk into our studio, and find nothing at all we'd have to apologize for.

No street noise creeping into a singer's whisper. No speakers that flatter you instead of telling the truth. No crackling headphones. No air conditioning that has to be switched off before every take.

Big artists will forgive a lot if a studio has character. What they won't forgive is a studio that wastes their time or lies to their ears.

There are five tests our studio has to pass, every single day, no exceptions.
### 1. Silence

The doors are closed. Nobody is playing an instrument.

You should hear almost nothing from outside. No traffic. No elevators. No air conditioning.

A soft vocal performance or a solo oud should sit against what we call a black background, not a gray one.

Gray means some unwanted noise has crept into the recording. Black is the silence we're after.
### 2. Honest Monitoring

The speakers in the control room, which the trade calls studio monitors, have to tell the truth.

If the bass sounds right here, it should sound right in a car on Cairo's Ring Road, in a pair of headphones in Dubai, or at a New Year's DJ set in Kuala Lumpur.

A control room that flatters you and fools your ears gets found out the moment the mix is played somewhere else.
### 3. A Live Room That Enhances Without Distorting

Musicians should really enjoy playing in this room, because of what it adds to the sound of their instruments.

Strings should feel open and wide. Drums should hit with real weight.

But the microphones also need to capture a sound that can still be shaped later, so the producer can tighten it up or bend it to what the music needs.

Beauty and accuracy are not opposites.

A musician can hear their instrument sounding beautiful, while the microphone captures it accurately at the very same moment.

Getting that balance takes more space and more careful design.
### 4. Technology You Can Depend On

Every input, every headphone line and every connection has to work, every day.

At this level, sessions run on tight schedules, sometimes down to the minute.

You can't leave a string section you've booked for the whole day sitting around while someone hunts for a missing cable.

And nobody wants to find a faulty cable just as the musicians are ready to play.
### 5. Discreet Hospitality

International artists can spend twelve hours a day in the studio, sometimes several days in a row.

They need somewhere to rest, recharge, make private calls, think, and make big decisions, away from visitors, cameras and attention.

People remember great studios not just for their microphones, but for how comfortable and welcome they felt working there.

Studios like Abbey Road, Capitol, Electric Lady and AIR are good reference points.

Not because we want to copy them, but because we're after the same people who treat those studios as the benchmark and quietly measure every new room against them.

Our goal is easy to write down and hard to pull off:

We want a producer who knows those legendary studios to leave Cairo without a single doubt about their session.
